Fire Hydrant vs Hose Reel: What Is the Difference?

The terms fire hydrant, landing valve and hose reel are sometimes used as if they mean the same thing. They are connected to water-based fire protection, but they are different pieces of equipment.

Published 4 November 2026 3 MIN READ LICENCE MFS/LA/RF-34/RD-35
Section Index ▾

A fire hydrant point, a landing valve and a hose reel can all be part of a building’s water-based fire protection arrangements. Their design, location and intended users are not identical. The approved fire plan and system drawings should show which equipment is installed and how it is meant to be used.

What is a fire hydrant?

A hydrant is an outlet connected to a fire-water supply. External yard hydrants are generally positioned around a site so responders can connect suitable hose equipment. Internal hydrant outlets may be provided through a riser system, with landing valves located at designated floors.

What is a landing valve?

A landing valve is a valve outlet on a riser, typically installed at a floor landing or designated location. It allows the appropriate hose to be connected to the building’s fire-water supply. Access should be kept clear, identification should be visible and the valve must be maintained as part of the system.

What is a hose reel?

A hose reel is a permanently arranged length of hose on a drum, generally intended for controlled use on a small fire by a person who has been trained and can operate it safely. It is not a substitute for evacuation, and it is not intended to tackle every fire type or a fire that is spreading.

How are these items maintained?

Checks should follow the installed system design, applicable Indian Standards and the manufacturer’s instructions. Facility teams should keep the cabinets accessible, look for visible damage and leakage, record defects and arrange competent testing and maintenance. Do not operate valves or discharge water as an informal test.

Which equipment does your building need?

That depends on occupancy, building height, approved fire plans, water supply and the applicable rules. Do not select equipment based only on a generic online comparison. A site-specific review should identify what is installed and whether it is being maintained correctly.
