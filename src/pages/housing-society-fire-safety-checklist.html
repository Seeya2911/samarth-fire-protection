A housing society committee can use this checklist to identify visible issues and organise follow-up work. It is not a statutory audit form and cannot replace inspection by a competent fire safety professional.

1. Keep escape routes clear

  • Keep staircases, corridors, exit doors and final exits free from storage and obstructions.
  • Do not lock or block fire exits in a way that prevents safe escape.
  • Check that fire doors close properly and are not wedged open.
  • Keep access for fire service vehicles and emergency responders clear.
  • Check that exit signs and emergency lights appear intact and arrange testing under the maintenance plan.

2. Check the fire safety equipment

Make a list of the equipment installed in the building. Depending on the approved plan, this may include fire pumps, hydrants, wet risers, landing valves, hose reels, sprinklers, extinguishers, fire alarm panels, detectors and manual call points. Check that the equipment is visible, accessible and not obviously damaged. Keep service and test records.

3. Review the pump room and water supply

Keep the pump room locked against unauthorised access but available to responsible personnel and emergency responders. Do not use it for storage. Review the pump and tank records, controller indications, visible leakage and any faults reported by the maintenance provider. Functional tests should be planned and carried out by competent personnel.

4. Check fire extinguishers

Confirm extinguishers are in their assigned locations, easy to reach, correctly identified and visibly in serviceable condition. Check their inspection tags and service records. The correct type and number depend on the risks and layout of the building; do not assume one extinguisher type is suitable for every area.

5. Look at everyday housekeeping and risks

  • Do not store combustible materials in staircases, lift lobbies, electrical rooms or pump rooms.
  • Keep electrical panels accessible and report damaged wiring, burning smells or unusual heating.
  • Manage hot work, temporary electrical work, waste and gas storage through a responsible process.
  • Keep parking areas, fire access routes and equipment cabinets free from obstructions.

6. Prepare residents, guards and staff

Make sure people know how to raise the alarm, which exits to use, where the assembly point is and whom to contact. Agree how assistance will be arranged for older residents, children and people who may need help using stairs. Train security staff to report faults and follow the building’s emergency plan rather than improvise repairs.

7. Keep the records together

Keep the approved fire plan, equipment list, AMC/work orders, pump and system test reports, extinguisher service tags, fault register, drill records and statutory documents in one place. Give the committee and facility manager a way to see which defects are still open.

8. Turn the checklist into action

For each problem, record the location, the risk, who will arrange the fix and by when. Prioritise anything that could affect escape or stop an installed life-safety system from working. Arrange repairs and testing through a competent provider.

Important note

Use the current Maharashtra fire-safety rules, the building’s approved fire plan and the competent authority’s directions for statutory requirements. The Maharashtra Fire & Emergency Services official website publishes the Act and amended Rules.
