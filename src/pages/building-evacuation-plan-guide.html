Evacuation plans fail when they are too complicated to remember under stress. These six steps cover the sequence that matters most — and each links to where you can go deeper.

1. Raise the alarm

Activate the nearest manual call point if one is available, shout to alert neighbours, and call the fire brigade on 101 (or 112) immediately — don't assume someone else already has.

2. Floor wardens act

Trained wardens sweep their assigned floor, direct people to the nearest safe exit, and close doors behind them as they leave — closed doors slow fire and smoke spread significantly.

3. Use the stairs — never lifts

Walk, don't run, to the nearest stairwell. Lifts can lose power or open directly onto a fire floor, and must never be used during an evacuation.

4. Go to the assembly point

The designated muster point should be clearly marked, known in advance by every resident or staff member, and positioned clear of building exits and the fire brigade's access route.

5. Take a headcount

Each warden confirms everyone from their floor or department is present at the assembly point, and reports any missing person to the fire brigade immediately on arrival — this single step is often what finds someone in time.

6. Brief the fire brigade

When the fire brigade arrives, someone familiar with the building should be ready to share the fire's location, any known hazards (gas cylinders, chemical storage), and where hydrants and risers are located.

A plan only works if it's practised

Wardens should be nominated and trained in advance, and the sequence rehearsed through regular drills — see our housing society fire drill guide for how to plan one.
