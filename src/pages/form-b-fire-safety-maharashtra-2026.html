Form B is not a general-purpose Fire NOC. It is a periodic certificate concerning the maintenance of the fire-prevention and life-safety measures in a building. Under the Maharashtra Rules, the licensed agency issues the certificate and the owner or occupier submits it to the Chief Fire Officer or Director, as applicable. Use the latest Directorate guidance, the applicable Rules and the approved fire plan when preparing the submission.

What changed in the 2026 guidance?

The Directorate of Maharashtra Fire & Emergency Services lists a circular titled Guidelines to issue Form B, dated 7 July 2026. The circular explains the six-month reporting periods and supporting evidence expected from the owner/occupier and licensed agency. It also explains that the maintenance schedule for individual equipment continues to apply during the Form B cycle; the certificate period does not replace the service intervals required by relevant standards.

Which period does each Form B cover?

Submission cycleMaintenance period described in the 2026 guidance
January1 July to 31 December of the preceding year
July1 January to 30 June of the current year

Confirm the current submission route, local process and any subsequent circular with the local Chief Fire Officer or nominated officer. Do not rely on an old form template or another building’s filing date as a substitute for current instructions.

What should you prepare before the inspection?

The July 2026 guidance lists supporting records that may include:

  • A covering letter from the owner or occupier.
  • The prescribed Form B and annexures for the active and passive systems installed in the building.
  • A copy of the AMC/CAMC or work order (the circular says the amount need not be shown in the copy submitted).
  • Service-checking and test reports for the relevant period, signed by the parties as required.
  • The latest site inspection report with the required digital signatures.
  • A copy of the licensed agency’s current Directorate licence. The digital signature on Form B should match the signature on the licence issued by the Director.
  • Date-stamped photographs showing the operation/testing of the relevant equipment, as requested by the 2026 guidance. Examples include pump operation and the relevant pressure-gauge readings; the required evidence depends on the systems installed at the site.
  • Any additional records requested by the local fire authority or the current circular, including applicable mock-drill evidence.

The exact list should be checked against the current circular and the systems installed at the property. A report should describe the work actually done. Do not create test records or sign off an inspection that did not take place.

The circular also recommends that, within one building or occupancy, water-based systems such as pumps, hydrants and sprinklers be maintained by one agency; detection/alarm/suppression systems by one agency; and passive measures such as fire doors, sealants, pressurisation and smoke management by one agency. This is intended to protect system integrity. Confirm the exact arrangement and any exceptions with the owner/occupier and the authority.

What should the maintenance inspection look at?

The equipment depends on the building. A site may have hydrants and wet risers, fire pumps, sprinklers, fire alarm and detection systems, extinguishers, smoke management, fire doors, seals or other passive measures. Each item needs the inspection or test appropriate to its standard, design and manufacturer instructions.

The Directorate’s July 2026 guidance refers to current Indian Standards for these systems, including standards for extinguishers, hydrants, pumps, hose reels, hoses, landing valves, sprinklers and fire alarm systems. A single generic checklist or one pressure value cannot cover every building.

Form B is not a one-day paperwork exercise

Maintenance has to happen throughout the year. Some equipment may have daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or yearly tasks depending on the applicable standard and site plan. The Form B cycle does not cancel the next maintenance date. Keep a defect register and close faults with evidence of repair and retesting.

Form A, Form B and Fire NOC are different

  • Form A: associated with installation of fire-prevention and life-safety measures.
  • Form B: periodic maintenance certificate for the measures installed.
  • Fire NOC / approval: a separate approval or clearance issued for the relevant stage and purpose.

Use the exact prescribed form and current digital-signature process. A quotation, AMC or service report is not itself a Form B certificate.
