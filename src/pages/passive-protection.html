Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the difference between active and passive fire protection?

Active fire protection systems require an action or trigger to operate (e.g. smoke triggering alarms, heat busting sprinklers, or operating fire extinguishers). Passive fire protection is structural and works without activation (e.g. fire doors containment, shaft mortar sealing, and fireproofing paints that delay fire spread).

Q: Why are electrical shafts considered high-risk areas in high-rises?

Electrical shafts run vertically through all floors of a building. If the gaps around the cables on each floor are not sealed with fire-resistant barrier materials, the shaft acts as a chimney. A fire in the basement will suck air and spread smoke and flames to the top floor within minutes.

Q: What are fire door ratings?

Fire door assemblies are tested and certified by laboratories to withstand heat, fire, and smoke penetration for specific durations. Common rating durations are 60 minutes, 90 minutes, and 120 minutes (1 or 2 hours), giving occupants time to evacuate safely.
