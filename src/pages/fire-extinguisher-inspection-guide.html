Fire Extinguisher Inspection: A Practical Checklist for Facilities

Portable extinguishers are a first-response measure for suitable incipient fires. They must be appropriate for the hazards present, correctly located, accessible and maintained. A quick visual check helps identify problems but does not replace professional servicing.

Check location and access

Confirm each extinguisher is in its designated location, visible, signed and reachable without moving stored items. Check that mounting brackets or cabinets are secure and that the route to the equipment is not obstructed.

Look for visible damage

Inspect the cylinder body for dents, corrosion, leakage, damaged hose or nozzle, missing pins, broken seals and illegible labels. Check that the operating instructions face outward and can be read. Remove damaged or suspect units from reliance and arrange professional assessment.

Review pressure and weight indicators

Where the extinguisher has a pressure gauge, check that the indicator is within the manufacturer's marked operating range. For units without a gauge, follow the manufacturer's prescribed checks. Weight checks, cartridge checks and internal examinations must be performed by trained personnel as required.

Confirm suitability for the hazard

Extinguisher type and rating must match the likely fire classes and the electrical, cooking, flammable-liquid or other hazards in the area. Do not select or use an extinguisher based only on colour or size. Follow the label and the building's fire strategy.

Keep service records current

Maintain an inventory with equipment ID, location, type, capacity, last service, next due date and defects. Keep service tags and reports legible. Service intervals and hydrostatic test requirements depend on the extinguisher type, applicable standard and manufacturer's instructions.

Train people to use extinguishers safely

Training should explain when to evacuate, how to raise the alarm, when an extinguisher may be used and when not to approach a fire. Never encourage untrained occupants to fight a developing fire or delay evacuation.

Frequently asked questions

How often should extinguishers be serviced?

Follow the applicable Indian standard, manufacturer's instructions and current maintenance requirements for the specific extinguisher type.

Can a gauge in the green zone prove an extinguisher is safe?

No. It is one visual indicator only. The cylinder, hose, seal, label, weight where applicable and service history also matter.

Can every extinguisher be used on electrical fires?

No. Check the extinguisher's marked rating and the hazards involved. Use only equipment suitable for the specific risk.

Need a site-specific fire safety assessment?

Requirements depend on the building, installed systems and applicable rules. Speak with the team about an assessment or maintenance visit.
