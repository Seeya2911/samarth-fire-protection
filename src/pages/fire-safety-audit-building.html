Fire Safety Audit for Buildings: What to Check and How to Prepare

A useful fire safety audit does more than count extinguishers. It checks whether the building's fire-protection measures are suitable, accessible, maintained and usable by the people who may need them in an emergency.

Start with the building and its risks

Review the building's use, height, occupancy, floor layout, areas with higher fire load, storage practices and changes since the last assessment. Compare what exists on site with the approved drawings and applicable requirements. A checklist copied from another building is not a substitute for a property-specific assessment.

Check escape routes and compartmentation

Walk the escape routes from occupied areas to safe exits. Look for locked or obstructed doors, storage in staircases, damaged exit hardware, missing signs, poor emergency lighting and fire doors that do not close correctly. Check that penetrations through fire-rated walls and floors have appropriate fire stopping.

Review active fire systems

Inspect the condition and accessibility of extinguishers, hose reels, hydrants, sprinkler controls, fire pumps, alarm panels, detectors, manual call points and emergency communication equipment. Where functional testing is required, it should be carried out by competent personnel using the appropriate procedure and safety controls.

Check records and maintenance evidence

Review maintenance schedules, service reports, pump test logs, alarm fault history, training attendance, drill observations, previous audit findings and evidence that defects were closed. A system that looks intact but has no reliable maintenance history deserves further investigation.

Prioritise findings

Record each finding with its location, risk, evidence, recommended action, responsible person and target date. Separate immediate life-safety issues from planned maintenance and longer-term upgrades. Track closure and verify that the correction actually resolves the problem.

Make the audit useful after the visit

Share a clear report with photographs and plain-language explanations. Assign actions to named owners, set realistic deadlines and schedule a follow-up. Use the findings to update training, evacuation arrangements and the preventive-maintenance plan.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a building have a fire safety audit?

The required frequency depends on the building type, applicable rules, authority directions and the scope of the engagement. Follow the current requirements for the premises.

Does an audit automatically certify compliance?

No. An audit identifies the status and findings. Any statutory certificate or approval follows its own prescribed process.

Who should conduct the audit?

Use competent fire-safety professionals with the appropriate licence or qualification for the work and building category.

Need a site-specific fire safety assessment?

Requirements depend on the building, installed systems and applicable rules. Speak with the team about an assessment or maintenance visit.
