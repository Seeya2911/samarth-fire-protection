An AMC is only useful when the building and the service provider agree what is covered. “Complete fire system maintenance” can mean different things from one contract to another. A good agreement identifies the equipment, schedule, service tasks, response arrangements and reporting in plain language.

1. List every system and asset included

Write down the systems, buildings, floors and equipment covered. This may include fire pumps, hydrants, wet risers, landing valves, hose reels, sprinklers, fire alarm panels, detectors, manual call points, extinguishers, gas suppression, fire doors and other passive measures. Identify any item that is excluded or maintained by another provider.

2. Agree the maintenance schedule

Different systems have different inspection and test intervals. The contract should state the task and frequency for each group of equipment, based on the applicable Indian Standard, approved fire plan, manufacturer instructions and site conditions. Do not assume that one monthly or quarterly visit automatically covers every legal or technical requirement.

3. Define tests and service activities

  • What visual inspections and functional checks will be completed?
  • Which pump runs, alarm tests, flow tests or other activities are included?
  • Which tasks need a separate shutdown, permit, water discharge or client approval?
  • Are refilling, HPT, spare parts and consumables included or quoted separately?
  • How will temporary impairments be controlled and reported?

4. Agree fault reporting and response arrangements

The contract should state how the building reports a fault, who receives it, the normal working hours, the response arrangements and what happens when a system is out of service. Avoid vague promises such as “24/7” unless the contract explains what the provider will actually deliver.

5. Ask for service reports and a defect register

Each visit should produce a record showing the date, equipment checked, tests carried out, findings, parts used, defects, recommended action and responsible person. Defects should remain open until the building has evidence of correction and any required retest.

6. Check how statutory documentation is handled

Clarify whether the AMC includes support for maintenance records and Form B preparation, and which licensed agency is responsible for any prescribed certificate. An AMC by itself does not guarantee that a building complies or that a statutory certificate can be issued. The equipment must be maintained and found in the condition required by the applicable rules and standards.

Before signing

Compare the scope against the building’s equipment register, approved plans, previous inspection reports and unresolved faults. Ask the provider to identify assumptions and exclusions in writing. This article is a purchasing checklist, not a template contract or a legal opinion.
