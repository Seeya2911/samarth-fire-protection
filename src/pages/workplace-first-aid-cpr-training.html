Training should match the workplace and the people taking part. A warehouse, office, school and housing society do not have the same layout or emergency arrangements. Plan the session around the site’s actual risks and approved emergency plan.

Fire safety awareness

Participants can learn how to recognise common fire hazards, raise the alarm, report a problem, follow exit signs, use the designated escape routes and reach the assembly point. Explain who coordinates the response and how visitors or contractors are included.

Fire extinguisher and equipment demonstrations

A supervised demonstration can help participants understand extinguisher labels, fire classes, operating instructions and safe handling. Practical use should only take place in a controlled exercise, with suitable equipment, a safe location and competent supervision. It should never encourage people to fight a fire instead of evacuating.

Evacuation drills and team roles

A drill can help test whether the alarm can be heard, the routes are clear, wardens understand their roles and people know where to assemble. Record the lessons and assign actions to address blocked routes, unclear instructions or other gaps.

First aid and CPR training

First aid and CPR sessions introduce participants to basic emergency response and practical skills under instructor supervision. The topics, depth and certificate (where applicable) depend on the course and training provider. Confirm the course outline, instructor credentials, assessment and certificate details before booking.

How should an organisation prepare?

  • Share the site layout, emergency plan and type of premises.
  • Estimate the number and roles of participants.
  • Identify language, accessibility or mobility needs.
  • Agree the training topics and whether practical demonstrations are included.
  • Choose a safe space and coordinate with the facility/security team.
  • Keep attendance and post-session action records.

Important note

Training is not a substitute for an approved emergency plan, working equipment or emergency services. In a real incident, raise the alarm, evacuate when required and contact emergency responders.
