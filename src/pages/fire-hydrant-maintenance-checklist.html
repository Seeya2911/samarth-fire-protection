Fire Hydrant and Hose Reel Maintenance Checklist for Buildings

A hydrant or hose-reel system is only useful when it can be reached, operated and supplied as intended. Maintenance should be planned, documented and performed by competent personnel using the building's approved system information.

Keep hydrant points accessible

Ensure landing valves, hose cabinets and hydrant outlets are visible and not blocked by vehicles, stored goods or temporary construction. Check that cabinet doors, locks, glass panels, signage and access paths are in serviceable condition.

Inspect hoses, nozzles and couplings

Look for visible cuts, cracks, corrosion, damaged threads, missing washers, distorted couplings and signs of leakage. Confirm that hoses and nozzles are present, compatible and stored correctly. Any pressure or flow test must follow the system design and applicable standard.

Check pumps and controls

Review the fire pump room for clear access, leaks, corrosion, unusual noise, vibration, damaged gauges and signs of overheating. Check controller indications and maintenance records. Pump start and performance tests should be conducted by trained personnel under the approved test procedure.

Review the water supply and valves

Confirm that the relevant tanks, valves and supply arrangements are identified and maintained. Check valve position indicators and records. Never operate an unfamiliar valve or isolate a fire-water supply without authorisation and an approved impairment plan.

Document defects and corrective work

Record the exact location, equipment identifier, observed defect, date, photographs, responsible contractor and closure evidence. Keep test reports and service records together so that the building team can see recurring issues and overdue work.

Manage system impairment safely

If a critical component must be taken out of service, notify the responsible building team, assess the risk, restrict hazardous activities where necessary and arrange appropriate temporary precautions. Restore the system promptly and document the return-to-service checks.

Frequently asked questions

Can a society test a hydrant system on its own?

Routine visual checks can be assigned to trained building staff, but functional and pressure tests should be performed by competent personnel using the approved procedure.

Is one pressure value suitable for every building?

No. Required pressures and flow performance depend on the system design, building conditions and applicable standards.

What if a hydrant valve is leaking?

Mark and report it, protect the area and arrange repair by a competent service provider. Do not leave a known defect unrecorded.

Need a site-specific fire safety assessment?

Requirements depend on the building, installed systems and applicable rules. Speak with the team about an assessment or maintenance visit.
