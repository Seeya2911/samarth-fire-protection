Terms are grouped by topic. Each links to a fuller guide where one exists.

Compliance & regulation

Form A
A certificate issued by a licensed fire agency confirming fire-protection systems have been installed and completed as per the approved plan. Read more in our Form B guide.
Form B
A statutory six-monthly certificate (filed in January and July) confirming that installed fire-safety systems remain operational and maintained, required under the Maharashtra Fire Prevention and Life Safety Measures Act, 2006.
NOC (No Objection Certificate)
A clearance issued by the fire department confirming no objection to a building's occupation, based on its fire-safety provisions at the time of inspection.
Class D Licensed Agency
A category of fire-safety agency licensed by the Directorate of Maharashtra Fire & Emergency Services to install, inspect and certify specific classes of fire-protection systems. Samarth Fire holds licence MFS/LA/RF-34/RD-35.
Competent authority
The government body — typically the Directorate of Maharashtra Fire & Emergency Services or local Chief Fire Officer — with legal authority over fire-safety approvals for a given area.

Fire-fighting systems

Wet riser
A vertical pipe kept permanently filled with pressurised water so a hose can be connected on any floor without delay. See the full wet riser guide.
Dry riser
A vertical pipe left empty until the fire brigade pumps water into it through a ground-level inlet, typically used where a permanent water supply is impractical.
Landing valve
The valve and outlet on each floor where a hose connects to the riser pipe.
Jockey pump
A small pump that maintains constant line pressure so the larger main and standby pumps don't start and stop for minor pressure drops.
Booster pump
A pump that increases water pressure to meet the flow and pressure required at the highest or most distant outlet in a system.
Yard hydrant / landing valve
An outdoor hydrant point, typically along a building's perimeter, giving the fire brigade external access to the water supply.

Detection & alarm

Conventional fire alarm panel
A panel that reports an alarm by zone (area), not by individual device. See conventional vs addressable.
Addressable fire alarm panel
A panel that identifies the exact device in alarm, down to its location, rather than just the zone.
Manual call point (MCP)
A break-glass or push-button unit that lets a person manually trigger the fire alarm.
Heat detector
A detector that triggers on a rapid rise in temperature or a fixed threshold, often used where smoke detectors would give frequent false alarms (kitchens, boiler rooms).

Extinguishers

HPT (Hydrostatic Pressure Testing)
A periodic pressure test of an extinguisher cylinder to confirm it can safely hold its rated pressure, required every 3–5 years depending on type under IS 2190.
ABC dry powder
A general-purpose extinguishing agent effective on Class A, B and C fires. See the extinguisher types guide.
P.A.S.S. technique
The standard method for operating a portable extinguisher: Pull the pin, Aim at the base of the fire, Squeeze the handle, Sweep side to side.

Passive protection

Fire-rated door (FRD)
A door assembly tested and certified to resist fire for a specific duration (e.g. 2-hour rated), typically fitted with an automatic door closer to stay shut.
Fire damper
A device inside ductwork that closes automatically in a fire to stop flame and smoke spreading through the ventilation system.
Penetration seal / fire stop
Fire-resistant sealant or collar used where pipes or cables pass through a fire-rated wall or floor, restoring the barrier's rating at that opening.
