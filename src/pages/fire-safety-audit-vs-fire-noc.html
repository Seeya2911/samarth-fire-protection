These terms are often mentioned together, but they refer to different things. A building may need an approval at a particular stage, ongoing maintenance of installed systems and separate reviews to identify gaps. The exact requirements depend on the site and applicable law.

What is a fire safety audit?

A fire safety audit is a review of the building’s risks, fire protection measures, escape routes, housekeeping and supporting records. It can identify equipment that is missing, inaccessible, damaged, overdue for service or not aligned with the approved plan. The scope should be agreed for the specific property.

What is a Fire NOC or fire approval?

A Fire NOC or fire approval is a separate clearance or approval from the competent authority for the relevant stage and purpose. The documents, drawings and inspections required depend on the building and the type of proposal. A maintenance report does not replace the approval process.

What are Form A and Form B?

  • Form A: associated with the installation of fire-prevention and life-safety measures.
  • Form B: periodic maintenance certificate relating to the systems installed in the building.

The Directorate issued updated Form B guidelines on 7 July 2026. Check the official circular list and the Acts and Rules page for current instructions.

Which one does your building need?

Start with the property’s approved fire plan, occupancy, current status and the issue you are trying to resolve. A building owner may need an approval, a periodic maintenance certificate, a site audit, a repair or more than one of these. Ask the competent authority or a currently licensed agency to clarify the requirement for the specific premises.
