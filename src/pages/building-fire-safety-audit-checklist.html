A fire safety audit is a structured review of the fire risks and protection arrangements at a particular property. The right checks vary between a housing society, office, school, hospital, warehouse or industrial site. Use this list to prepare for a review, not as a self-issued certificate of compliance.

1. Start with the building and its approved plan

Record the use of the building, height, floor count, basements, high-risk rooms, major changes in layout and any change of occupancy. Gather available approved fire plans, fire approvals, system drawings and previous inspection reports. The actual requirement depends on the site and the current rules.

2. Check escape routes and exits

  • Are corridors, staircases and final exits clear of storage and obstructions?
  • Do exit doors open and operate as intended and are fire doors able to close?
  • Are escape routes and exits clearly marked?
  • Does emergency lighting appear present and in serviceable condition?
  • Are assembly points and access routes for emergency responders kept clear?
  • Are arrangements in place for people who may need help during evacuation?

3. Review active fire protection systems

Check the systems provided for the building: fire pumps, hydrants and risers, landing valves, hose reels, sprinklers, extinguishers, fire alarm panels, detectors, manual call points and any special suppression system. Look for visible damage, missing equipment, leakage, inaccessible cabinets, recurring alarms and records of testing or maintenance.

4. Review passive fire protection

Look for fire doors that are wedged open or damaged, missing door closers, unsealed service penetrations, damaged fire barriers and any change that may have compromised a protected shaft or compartment. Repairs or alterations should use products and methods suitable for the specific fire-rated construction and approved design.

5. Check housekeeping and common fire risks

  • Combustible storage in staircases, electrical rooms and pump rooms.
  • Damaged cables, overloaded sockets or signs of overheating that need qualified electrical attention.
  • Temporary wiring, hot-work activity, gas cylinders or flammable storage relevant to the site.
  • Uncontrolled parking or storage that blocks fire access routes and equipment.
  • Fire doors, ducts and service openings affected by recent renovation work.

6. Check people, training and records

Confirm who is responsible for raising an alarm, guiding occupants, checking assigned areas and meeting emergency responders. Review resident or staff training, fire drill reports, equipment demonstrations, service reports, fault logs and current emergency contact details.

7. Convert findings into an action plan

For every defect, record the location, evidence, risk, immediate action, permanent correction, responsible person and target date. Track urgent issues until they are repaired and, where necessary, tested again. If a life-safety system is impaired, use the building’s approved impairment procedure and inform the responsible manager.

Audit, statutory inspection and certification are not interchangeable

A site review can identify risks and maintenance gaps, but it does not automatically replace statutory approvals or prescribed certificates. In Maharashtra, Form A and Form B have specific roles under the fire-safety framework. Check the official Acts and Rules and the latest Directorate circulars for current requirements.
